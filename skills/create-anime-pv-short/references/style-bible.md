# Anime PV Motion Style Bible

## Style identity

Use the combination **Japanese anime PV + editorial graphic design + AE-style motion graphics + audiovisual beat synchronization**.

The result should feel like a premium anime ending sequence, character concept film, fashion editorial, or experimental title sequence. Preserve 2D flatness and graphic control. The style supports a story but must not replace it.

## Visual grammar

- High-quality 2D cel-shaded anime drawing with stable, clean contours.
- Deep black and midnight blue foundations; electric cyan, vermilion red, and ivory accents.
- Large negative space and asymmetric editorial composition.
- Bold rectangles, circles, diagonal planes, thin grids, abstract marks, silhouettes, and cropped character details.
- Close-ups of eyes, profile, hair, hands, clothing edges, props, and full-body silhouettes used as rhythmic visual fragments.
- Restrained screen-print grain, paper texture, film noise, subtle registration offset, and soft bloom.
- Clean value hierarchy: one dominant subject, one graphic motif, one accent color per frame.

Adapt the palette to the user's story while keeping the same limited-color discipline. Choose one recurring motif from the story—moon, ring, train window, red thread, falling paper, blade arc, raindrop—and reuse its shape across shots.

## Motion grammar

- Match cuts based on shape, color, silhouette, gaze, action, or screen direction.
- Geometric masks, color-plane wipes, foreground occlusion, parallax, restrained push/pull, lateral whip, rack focus, and eased speed ramps.
- Transition out of an object already present in the frame. Every transition must have a visible motivation.
- Alternate dense rhythmic fragments with a held hero frame.
- Synchronize cuts, graphic impacts, and color changes to music beats.
- Keep character motion economical: turn, glance, reach, step, hair or clothing follow-through. Let composition and transition carry energy.

## Story integration

For 5–15 seconds, express one readable emotional change, such as:

- hesitation to decision;
- separation to recognition;
- pursuit to confrontation;
- fear to defiance;
- memory to acceptance;
- calm to sudden revelation.

Show cause and effect with visual anchors. A prop or motif introduced early should trigger the final image or transition. Prefer one protagonist, one location family, and one decisive action.

## Prompt language block

Blend the following ideas naturally into the final prose rather than dumping them as a keyword list:

> premium Japanese anime PV, high-quality 2D cel animation, modern editorial graphic design, sophisticated motion graphics, deep black and midnight blue, limited electric cyan/vermilion/ivory accents, strong negative space, asymmetric magazine layout, geometric masks, shape match cuts, silhouette transitions, foreground wipes, parallax, smooth easing curves, restrained screen-print grain, subtle film noise, precise audiovisual synchronization, emotional anime ending atmosphere

## Sound grammar

- Use Japanese electronic pop, dream pop, ambient synth, restrained breakbeat, or cinematic electronic music according to the emotion.
- Add subtle whooshes, paper friction, electronic clicks, cloth movement, environmental ambience, and one restrained low-frequency impact.
- Use silence or ambience around the final emotional frame when appropriate.
- Do not make every cut loud.

## Standard negative constraints

Exclude: photoreal live action, heavy 3D plastic rendering, cheap game-CG look, uncontrolled neon cyberpunk, cluttered backgrounds, random morphing, unmotivated camera spins, excessive effects, facial drift, identity changes, costume changes, malformed hands, inconsistent props, unstable line art, muddy colors, illegible generated text, watermarks, oversharpening, low resolution, cuts unrelated to music, and incoherent screen direction.

Add concept-specific negatives when needed.

## Prompt skeleton

Use this as a reasoning scaffold, not a rigid form:

1. State duration, aspect ratio, story beat, protagonist, and setting.
2. Lock character appearance and recurring motif.
3. Describe the chronological shot sequence with exact time ranges.
4. Specify the visual grammar and limited palette.
5. Specify camera movement and the motivation for each transition.
6. Specify music, sound effects, and beat synchronization.
7. End with consistency, 2D-quality, and exclusion requirements.
