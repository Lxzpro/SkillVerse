# Reality-Aligned Atmosphere Framework

Use this framework to control mood without turning a prompt into disconnected adjective soup.

## Alignment rules

1. Anchor every choice to one physical place, time, weather state, and camera position.
2. Keep detail granularity consistent. Do not specify one dimension at cinematography level while leaving a conflicting dimension as a vague mood word.
3. Express cause and effect. Light affects exposure and color; weather affects air, hair, clothing, surfaces, movement, and visibility; motion affects pose, fabric, hair, focus, and shutter appearance.
4. Use 4–8 decisive dimensions, not all options. Omit neutral dimensions rather than filling them mechanically.
5. Prefer observable image consequences over abstract labels. Translate `忧郁` into restrained expression, distance, low energy, cooler subdued color, negative space, or slow environmental rhythm as appropriate.
6. Keep the chosen reality level consistent. Documentary photography tolerates only plausible artifacts; surreal work may bend physics deliberately but should still have an internally coherent visual rule.

## Dimensions

### 1. Light and shadow

Control form, focus, and emotional pressure through light quality, key direction, contrast, source, pattern, and exposure.

- quality: soft / hard;
- key level: high-key / normal / low-key;
- direction: frontal, side, back, overhead, underlight;
- source: daylight, window, studio fixture, candle, neon, moonlight;
- pattern: dappled, rim light, silhouette, volumetric beam, blind shadows;
- exposure: airy, balanced, dim, locally clipped.

Reality check: name or imply where the light is located and let shadows, catchlights, background exposure, and skin highlights agree.

### 2. Color atmosphere

Specify temperature, saturation, value, and relationships rather than listing colors alone.

- temperature: cool / warm / mixed-source;
- saturation: restrained gray / natural / vivid;
- value: light / midtone / deep;
- relationship: monochrome, analogous, warm-cool contrast, complementary;
- palette examples: cyan-orange, blue-violet, gold, deep green, black-red, pink-white.

Reality check: derive dominant color from light sources, surfaces, weather, wardrobe, or deliberate grading. Avoid simultaneous unrelated palette labels.

### 3. Space

Define openness, focus depth, interpersonal distance, air, order, and subject-environment relationship.

- open / enclosed;
- deep focus / shallow focus;
- intimate / observational;
- clear air, haze, dust, humidity, smoke;
- minimal / crowded;
- integrated with environment / resisting environment.

Reality check: lens, aperture, distance, haze, and background scale must support the claimed space.

### 4. Emotional tension

Describe temperature, energy, tension, safety, relationship, and expressive intensity.

- warm / cold;
- calm / intense;
- loose / tense;
- safe / uneasy;
- intimate / distant;
- restrained / theatrical.

Translate emotions such as loneliness, ambiguity, solemnity, freedom, or oppression into visible expression, posture, distance, lighting, rhythm, and composition.

### 5. Time

Choose time of day, season, era, temporal state, and degree of wear only when relevant.

- dawn, morning, noon, sunset, blue hour, late night;
- spring lightness, summer heat, autumn decline, winter cold;
- contemporary, Y2K, 1990s, film-era nostalgia, future;
- candid instant, frozen moment, long wait, memory fragment;
- new / weathered.

Reality check: sun angle, sky value, practical lights, clothing, vegetation, skin response, and activity must agree with the time.

### 6. Weather and natural media

Use sun, cloud, rain, snow, fog, wind, dust, vapor, smoke, or petals to make atmosphere visible.

Reality check: show consequences on hair, clothing, skin, ground, glass, visibility, posture, and movement. State whether the subject enjoys, resists, waits in, or is overwhelmed by the weather.

### 7. Material and touch

Control tactile contrast: soft / rough, smooth / grainy, clean / mottled, light / heavy.

Use silk, leather, metal, glass, concrete, wood, gauze, film grain, digital sharpness, soft focus, fading, light leak, or frosted texture selectively.

Reality check: let material respond correctly to light, gravity, moisture, wear, and contact. Keep image-medium artifacts subordinate to the depicted materials.

### 8. Motion

Choose still / dynamic, stable / off-balance, extended / contracted, and a capture mode: frozen action, high-speed capture, natural motion, or slow-shutter trail.

Possible sources include hair, hems, rain, crowds, traffic, and camera shake. Set a coherent rhythm: slow, buoyant, urgent, or chaotic.

Reality check: shutter appearance, body mechanics, fabric, hair, droplets, and background movement must share the same motion logic.

### 9. Composition and visual pressure

Control symmetry, placement, negative space, viewpoint, horizon, crop, occlusion, and breathing room.

- symmetrical / asymmetric;
- centered / offset;
- spacious / frame-filled;
- eye-level / high / low;
- level / canted;
- normal distance / extreme close-up;
- full figure / partial occlusion;
- breathable / compressed.

Reality check: composition must be possible from the stated camera position and should reinforce, not merely label, the emotional relation.

### 10. Reality level

Set documentary / surreal, natural / theatrical, everyday / ritualized, credible / dreamlike, minimal / spectacular.

Choose a representation mode: real photograph, fashion editorial, film still, stage scene, or fantasy illustration. Use one primary mode unless hybridization is intentional and explained.

### 11. Narrative atmosphere

Define what seems to be happening: meeting, waiting, parting, fleeing, celebrating, confronting, being alone, or remembering.

Choose before / during / just after, clear / open-ended, observer / participant / survivor / outsider, and private instant / large event.

Reality check: include small evidence of the event rather than explaining the whole story in prose.

### 12. Overall character

Use 1–2 summary labels only after concrete choices are set: clear and healing; cold and restrained; romantic and ambiguous; hot and restless; oppressive and uneasy; solemn and epic; faded nostalgia; futuristic; minimal and rational; rough documentary realism.

## Assembly pattern

`[light] + [color] + [space] + [emotional tension] + [time/weather] + [material] + [motion] + [reality level]`

Example:

`Low-key side-backlight + cool gray low saturation + enclosed late-night interior + restrained tension + rain outside + wet glass and worn fabric + slight handheld motion softness + cinematic realism.`

Expanded physical logic:

`A late-night subject beside a rain-streaked window, weak cool street light forming a narrow side rim while a dim warm practical lamp leaves the far cheek in soft shadow; subdued cool-gray palette with a small amber contrast; enclosed room and shallow focus; restrained posture and gaze held just past the camera; humid glass, slightly damp loose hair near the temple, darkened fabric at the shoulder; faint handheld softness while the subject remains mostly still; cinematic but physically credible exposure and skin texture.`
